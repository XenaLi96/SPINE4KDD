\begin{abstract}
Spatial perturbation assays preserve tissue architecture, but their observed
neighbor responses conflate endogenous niche variation with
perturbation-specific propagation. We ask how much of a spatial response field
is context-explainable, where perturbation-conditioned signal remains, and
whether the remaining structure can support lower-burden intervention
prioritization. We construct Pre--Post Perturbation Pairing (\ppp), an audited
matched-control benchmark from Spatial Perturb-seq, and explicitly treat its
responses as guide-consistent pseudo-targets rather than observed causal
counterfactuals. Our analysis shows that tissue context and source-region
geometry explain most flat neighbor correlation, whereas correct direct-effect
profiles primarily improve radial organization: on block-held-out data,
context-only, shuffled-profile, and measured-profile models obtain neighbor/ring
Pearson correlations of $0.610/0.405$, $0.679/0.405$, and $0.684/0.457$.
\methodname{} models this separation with a context-explainable branch and a
directed perturbation-conditioned residual branch. It reaches $0.684/0.457$ on
Spatial Perturb-seq, $0.596/0.451$ under grouped guide holdout with measured
seeds, and program-level agreement up to $0.853$ on Perturb-map. Finally,
frozen held-out response fields support retrospective intervention
prioritization. Across 36 target specifications, a target-retaining operating
point achieves target-qualified rate $0.639$ while reducing collateral burden
by $0.354$ (95\% CI $0.222$--$0.476$); three pre-specified examples retain
$89.3$--$99.8\%$ of target utility while reducing burden by $24.4$--$49.7\%$.
Perturb-FISH provides directionally consistent native-panel support. These
results position spatial perturbation modeling as context-aware response-field
decomposition and experimental prioritization, not causal or clinical
prediction.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Spatial functional genomics places genetic perturbations within intact tissue
and measures molecular changes in situ
\citep{dhainaut2022spatialcrispr,binan2025perturbfish,shen2026spatialperturbseq}.
Unlike dissociated Perturb-seq, these assays can reveal whether a local
intervention remains confined to its intended niche or reorganizes distant and
off-program responses. Their cost and sparsity, however, make it impractical to
measure every perturbation in every tissue context. Predictive models could
help prioritize experiments, but only if their spatial signal is genuinely
perturbation-specific.

This requirement creates a fundamental evaluation problem. A high correlation
between predicted and observed neighbor expression can arise from reconstructing
stable anatomy, cell identity, or local composition rather than learning
propagation. Spatial variance and multiview models have established that
intrinsic and environmental components explain substantial spatial expression
variation \citep{arnol2019svca,tanevski2022misty,fischer2023ncem}. In a
perturbation assay, the measured neighbor field therefore mixes at least two
sources: a context-explainable component and a perturbation-conditioned
residual. Flat cell--gene Pearson correlation can obscure this distinction,
especially when stable or high-variance genes dominate the average.

We organize the study around four questions. \textbf{RQ1:} How much of the
observed neighbor response is explained by tissue context and source-region
geometry? \textbf{RQ2:} Where does perturbation-specific signal remain after
conditioning on context? \textbf{RQ3:} Can that residual structure be predicted
across held-out regions, perturbations, and assays? \textbf{RQ4:} Can predicted
fields prioritize perturbations that preserve a requested local response while
reducing far-field and off-program burden?

Our results support a more specific scientific conclusion than ``the full field
is predictable'': spatial perturbation fields are
\emph{context-dominated globally but perturbation-structured locally}.
Context-only prediction reaches neighbor/ring Pearson $0.610/0.405$ on
block-held-out Spatial Perturb-seq. Providing the correct measured direct-effect
profile changes flat neighbor correlation only from $0.679$ for a shuffled
profile to $0.684$, but changes ring correlation from $0.405$ to $0.457$.
Thus, perturbation identity contributes most clearly through radial and
program-selective organization rather than the bulk neighbor average.

This study contributes: (1) a quantitative characterization of
context-explainable and perturbation-conditioned spatial response fields; (2)
\ppp, an audited pseudo-counterfactual benchmark whose provenance and
limitations are explicit; (3) \methodname{}, a parsimonious predictive
decomposition with a context branch and a directed residual branch; and (4) a
retrospective, spatially constrained intervention-prioritization task showing
that perturbations with similar local utility can have different collateral
burdens.

\section{Related Work}
\textbf{Context in spatial molecular data.}
SVCA decomposes spatial molecular variation into intrinsic, environmental, and
cell--cell interaction components \citep{arnol2019svca}; MISTy models
intraview, juxtaview, and paraview effects
\citep{tanevski2022misty}; and NCEM predicts gene expression from a spatial
graph and niche composition \citep{fischer2023ncem}. These methods motivate our
context audit, but do not condition a cell-resolved response field on a direct
perturbation profile.

\textbf{Perturbation models.}
Dissociated-cell methods learn conditional translations, transport maps,
gene graphs, or disentangled representations
\citep{lotfollahi2019scgen,Lotfollahi2023,bunne2023cellot,roohani2023gears,piran2024biolord}.
They provide useful direct-effect priors but remove the tissue graph. Spatial
functional assays recover that graph
\citep{dhainaut2022spatialcrispr,binan2025perturbfish,shen2026spatialperturbseq}.
Recent methods such as SpatialProp, Celcomen, and CONCERT connect perturbations
to spatial context \citep{spatialprop2025,celcomen2026,concert2025}.
\methodname{} focuses on a complementary interface: given a measured or
predicted direct-effect profile and region, estimate signed receiver fields and
audit how much performance exceeds context and seed nulls.

\section{Problem and Benchmark}
\label{sec:data_construction}
\subsection{Spatial response fields}
Let $G=(V,E)$ be a local tissue graph, $X^{\mathrm{pre}}$ its estimated
pre-perturbation expression, $m_i$ an indicator of direct-effect membership,
and $s_i$ the measured or predicted direct-effect profile. The target is a
signed response field
\[
\Delta_i=X_i^{\mathrm{post}}-X_i^{\mathrm{pre}},\qquad i\in V.
\]
We model
\begin{equation}
\widehat\Delta_i=\widehat C_i+\widehat P_i,
\label{eq:predictive_decomposition}
\end{equation}
Here, $\widehat C_i$ is context-explainable variation. The term
$\widehat P_i$ is a perturbation-conditioned residual.
Equation~\eqref{eq:predictive_decomposition} is a predictive decomposition. It
does not identify a causal communication pathway or establish that every
residual change was transmitted from the direct-effect cells.

\subsection{Pre--Post Perturbation Pairing}
\label{sec:ppp_construction}
Spatial Perturb-seq does not observe the same cell before and after
perturbation. \ppp therefore constructs a pseudo-pre-state. For each perturbed
anchor, we form a 64-cell post patch and rank control-like anchors using spatial
distance, center-expression similarity, patch-expression similarity, and
cell-type composition. The top $K=3$ control patches are softly aligned into
the post-patch coordinate frame, converted to post-minus-pre deltas, and
aggregated with matching-score weights. Spatial partitions are assigned before
matching so that post anchors, controls, and derived targets do not cross
train/validation/test boundaries.

The final primary split contains 2,179 training, 2,179 validation, and 928 test
patches, with 512 predicted genes, six coarse cell types, and 23 edge features.
Multi-control consensus and ring consistency are
$0.944/0.923$. Across 512 genes, own-guide versus wrong-guide alignment is
$0.317$ versus $0.129$ at the center, $0.022$ versus $0.004$ in
high-confidence receivers, and $0.029$ versus $0.001$ over all receivers.
Because expression and composition are used both for matching and as model
inputs, \ppp is a guide-consistent pseudo-target rather than causal ground
truth. Gene-disjoint and no-expression matching variants remain required to
fully decouple target construction from prediction inputs.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}
\subsection{\methodname{} core}
The context branch receives baseline expression, cell state, position, and
local edges, while direct-effect indicators and profiles are masked:
\begin{align*}
h_i^{(0)}
&=\phi_x(X_i^{\mathrm{pre}})+\phi_c(c_i)+\phi_p(p_i),\\
\widehat C_i
&=\psi_C\!\left(\operatorname{GNN}_C(G,h^{(0)})_i\right).
\end{align*}
It is cross-fitted by split and then frozen. The residual target is
$R_i=\Delta_i-\widehat C_i$. The perturbation branch initializes each node from
its context embedding, direct-effect membership, and direct-effect profile,
\[
u_i^{(0)}=\phi_h(h_i)+\phi_m(m_i)+m_i\phi_s(s_i),
\]
and performs edge-aware message passing restricted first to
direct-effect-to-receiver edges and then to receiver-to-receiver paths:
\[
\widehat P_i=\psi_P\!\left(
\operatorname{GNN}_P(G,u^{(0)},e_{ij})_i\right).
\]
Distance/ring encodings, cell-state pairs, and communication features are edge
attributes. This routing is designed to retain the direction and radial
organization of the seed rather than applying unrestricted graph smoothing.
The final field is given by \Cref{eq:predictive_decomposition}.

The core objective combines robust node regression, radial summaries,
receiver-centered profiles, seed consistency, and residual regularization:
\begin{align*}
\mathcal L
={}&\lambda_{\mathrm{node}}\mathcal L_{\mathrm{node}}
+\lambda_{\mathrm{ring}}\mathcal L_{\mathrm{ring}}
+\lambda_{\mathrm{ctr}}\mathcal L_{\mathrm{centered}}\\
&+\lambda_{\mathrm{seed}}\mathcal L_{\mathrm{seed}}
+\lambda_{\mathrm{reg}}\|\widehat P\|_2^2.
\end{align*}
An optional reference-ST encoder and bounded radial refinement are used only in
the refined model. They are pretrained from $392{,}962$ unperturbed mouse-brain
cells ($1{,}619$ anchors; $9{,}316$ local pairs) and improve ring Pearson from
$0.452$ to $0.457$ while leaving neighbor Pearson essentially unchanged. We
therefore treat them as modest refinements, not the main source of performance.
Full equations and optimization details are in the appendix.

\subsection{Spatially constrained perturbation prioritization}
\label{sec:intervention_method}
For candidate action $a$, target program $t$, and requested ring $r$, target
utility is the signed target-program response $U(a;t,r)$. Collateral burden
combines pre-specified components:
\begin{equation}
S(a;t,r)=
\lambda_{\mathrm{far}}S_{\mathrm{far}}+
\lambda_{\mathrm{off}}S_{\mathrm{off}}+
\lambda_{\mathrm{undes}}S_{\mathrm{undes}},
\label{eq:burden}
\end{equation}
where the terms measure far-field target response, off-program response, and
response in pre-specified undesirable programs. Program definitions, weights,
and operating points are selected on validation data and frozen for test
evaluation. The constrained selector solves
\begin{equation}
a^\star=\arg\min_a \operatorname{UCB}\!\left[\widehat S(a)\right]
\quad\text{s.t.}\quad
\operatorname{LCB}\!\left[\widehat U(a)\right]\geq\tau_q.
\label{eq:selector}
\end{equation}
We also evaluate clean-score and rank-consensus variants because absolute
target calibration is incomplete. This is retrospective experimental
prioritization: it neither estimates clinical safety nor recommends treatment.

\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}
\subsection{Evaluation design}
The primary benchmark is mouse-brain Spatial Perturb-seq with held-out spatial
blocks. A grouped guide-out split jointly holds out \textit{fasn},
\textit{stk39}, and \textit{trem2}; measured-seed evaluation in this split tests
propagation transfer, whereas predicted-seed evaluation is the end-to-end task.
A controlled synthetic ligand--receptor benchmark uses 432 graphs from external
in vivo Perturb-seq signatures and NicheNet priors. Perturb-map supplies
spot/lesion-level external program readouts, and Perturb-FISH supplies a
panel-based external prioritization audit.

We report neighbor and ring Pearson, receiver-centered Pearson, and MAE for
response fields. For prioritization, we report target-qualified rate (TQR),
observed burden reduction relative to target-only, regret to the observed-field
oracle, low-burden success among five candidates (LB@5), and pairwise
lower-burden agreement. Confidence intervals resample target specifications;
the null probability is computed by 2,000 target-specification bootstrap
comparisons. Complete gene distributions, baseline definitions, split
manifests, synthetic tests, and external-assay details are in the appendix.

\Cref{tab:context_perturbation} changes the interpretation of the benchmark.
Context alone already explains most of the ordinary neighbor correlation.
Retaining the direct-effect region but shuffling its gene profile increases
neighbor correlation to $0.679$ without improving ring organization. The
correct measured profile reaches only $0.684$ neighbor correlation, yet raises
ring correlation from $0.405$ to $0.457$. Thus, source location and tissue
context dominate the flat metric, whereas perturbation identity is expressed
more clearly in radial structure. The same pattern appears under grouped guide
holdout: zero and observed seeds both yield neighbor $r=0.596$, while ring $r$
changes from $0.437$ to $0.451$.

This finding also explains why removing context residualization collapses the
model to $0.165/0.153$: the residual branch is not a replacement for modeling
the tissue state. Removing edge attributes gives $0.606/0.418$, and removing
direct-effect input gives $0.614/0.441$. The result is therefore a separation
of responsibilities: context reconstructs global variation, while
perturbation-conditioned, edge-aware routing contributes the spatially
organized residual.

the limiting factor.

Three pre-specified examples make the trade-off concrete. For a
TGF-$\beta$/stroma near-down objective, \texttt{sgrna\_oligo2} retains
$97.9\%$ of the target-only utility ($4.968$ versus $5.074$) while reducing
weighted burden from $1.188$ to $0.597$ ($49.7\%$). Vascular-stress near-down

reducing burden by $24.4\%$ each. Full far-field, off-program, and
pre-specified-undesirable-program decompositions are in the appendix.

Perturb-FISH tests whether the prioritization signal survives a different,
panel-limited spatial assay. On 39 validation-defined reliable
perturbation--context specifications, predicted utility plus predicted burden
gives TQR $0.718$, observed burden reduction $0.151$, and pairwise
lower-burden agreement $0.874$. Holding observed utility fixed and ranking only
by predicted burden raises reduction to $0.207$, locating the remaining
bottleneck in target-retention prediction rather than burden ranking.

The limitation is clearest under strict unseen-guide selection: TQR falls to
$0.333$ and burden reduction to $0.068$ despite pairwise agreement up to
$0.621$. We therefore claim retrospective lower-burden prioritization on
held-out spatial fields, not a solved end-to-end controller for unseen
perturbations.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion_conclusion}
The main scientific result is a decomposition of where predictive signal
comes from. In Spatial Perturb-seq, tissue context and source-region geometry
explain most flat neighbor variation. Perturbation-specific information is
weaker but reproducible in radial organization, selected programs, and
matched-utility burden differences. This reconciles the strong context-only
baseline with the non-zero correct-profile ring lift: the two results describe
different parts of the response field rather than contradicting each other.

\methodname{} is useful because it operationalizes this separation. The core
model is deliberately limited to a masked context branch and a directed
residual branch. Reference-ST pretraining and ring refinement provide a modest
incremental gain and are optional. The prioritization analysis then converts
predicted fields into a scientific decision problem: among actions expected to
meet a local objective, which one has lower far-field and off-program burden?
The positive block-held-out and Perturb-FISH results justify further
development, while the strict guide-out result identifies target calibration
as the next technical priority.

\section{Limitations and Ethical Considerations}
\label{sec:limitations_ethical_considerations}
\ppp responses are stable, guide-consistent pseudo-targets, not observed
counterfactual outcomes for the same cells. Its matching channels overlap
model inputs; no-expression and gene-disjoint pairing audits are still needed.
The present patch size (64 cells) and $K=3$ matched controls are fixed design
choices whose 32/64/128 and $K=1/3/5$ sensitivities have not yet been completed.
Grouped guide holdout with measured seeds establishes propagation transfer, not
end-to-end prediction of unseen perturbations; predicted-seed calibration and
multi-fold guide OOD remain incomplete. Perturb-map and Perturb-FISH provide
program- or panel-level external support rather than cell-resolved replication.
The IFN-$\gamma$ deployment study in the appendix is a plausibility check
without paired spatial ground truth.

\methodname{} and its prioritization outputs are for experimental hypothesis
generation, not clinical decision making, treatment recommendation, or
therapeutic safety assessment. Public or de-identified molecular data can
still contain sensitive tissue, disease, and donor information; downstream
use must follow the original consent, IRB, and data-use terms. Current
benchmarks overrepresent mouse brain, abundant cell states, selected guides,
and public assays. Prospective validation, broader tissues and species, and

Generative AI tools assisted language editing and code development. The authors
independently checked all designs, results, figures, and interpretations. No
generative AI model constructed benchmark targets or experimental labels.
